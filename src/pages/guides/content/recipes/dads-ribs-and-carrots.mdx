Dad’s pork ribs with carrots. The first part is mostly waiting; the last part is very much **do not stop stirring**.

## Ingredients

- Vegetable oil
- Pork spare ribs or back ribs, cut into smaller pieces
- Carrots
- Ginger
- Lal Mitch red chili powder
- Suraj ground black pepper
- Dark soy sauce — approximately a spatulaful
- Salt — **6 g per lb of meat**
- Water, enough to cover the ribs

<Callout title="Measurements from the family notes">

There’s no fixed batch size here. Scale the salt with the meat’s weight; the other amounts are by feel. The small spoon used in the original notes held roughly 1 g of salt, but spoon sizes vary, so weighing is more reliable.

</Callout>

## Preparation

1. Cut the ribs into smaller pieces with a cleaver, or ask the butcher to cut them.
2. Place the meat in a pot and add enough water to cover it.
3. Bring the water to a boil. Skim off the foam that rises to the surface.
4. Drain carefully.
5. Add fresh water and repeat the blanching and draining once more, as in Dad’s original method.
6. Wash and cut the carrots. Prepare the ginger.

## Cooking

1. Add vegetable oil to the bottom of the pot.
2. Warm the oil. Dad’s notes specify **220°F / about 104°C** for this step. If the oil is already hot from earlier cooking, check its temperature before continuing.
3. Add ginger.
4. Add red chili powder for colour.
5. Mix everything a little.
6. Add the pork.
7. Stir until evenly seasoned.
8. Add salt at **6 g per lb of meat**.
9. Stir again to distribute the salt.
10. Add dark soy sauce — approximately a spatulaful.
11. Add enough water to cover the meat.
12. Stir the ribs around a little.
13. Bring the water to a boil, then cover and maintain a steady simmer for **30 minutes**. Dad uses medium heat; adjust your stove so it simmers without boiling over.
14. Add the carrots.
15. Stir to distribute them evenly.
16. Cover again and cook for another **25 minutes**, until the ribs and carrots are tender.
17. If water remains, uncover and increase the heat to reduce it until the sauce coats the meat. **Stir constantly and lower the heat as it dries out to avoid burning.**
18. Add black pepper.
19. Stir until evenly mixed.
20. Take the meat out, let it rest for about **10 minutes**, and serve. :)

<Callout title="Before serving">

Check the thickest part of the pork, away from the bone, reaches **71°C / 160°F**. Continue cooking if needed; tenderness may take longer than the listed times. [Health Canada’s cooking temperatures](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/general-food-safety-tips/safe-internal-cooking-temperatures.html).

</Callout>

## Variations

- Swap the carrots for potatoes or beans. Adjust their cooking time as needed.
- Add a little extra soy sauce for a different flavour. Remember that it also adds salt.
