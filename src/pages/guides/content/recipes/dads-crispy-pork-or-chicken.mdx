One coating, two options: pork ribs or chicken. These quantities are for **~4 lb of meat**.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount / notes |
| --- | --- |
| Pork spare/back ribs **or** chicken | 4 lb; chicken drumsticks, thighs, or wings |
| Vegetable oil | Dad used Unico; his large-pot batch used about 3 L, but the amount must fit your equipment’s safe capacity |
| Cooking wine | 1 capful |
| Black pepper | 1.5 g |
| Salt | 6 g per lb of meat — **24 g for this batch** |
| Garlic powder | A little, optional |
| Tapioca starch | About **126 g**, roughly 10% of the meat’s weight |
| Egg | Start with **2**; add more if the coating mixture is too dry |

Use vegetable oil suitable for frying. The relevant property is its **smoke point**, rather than its boiling point.

## Preparation

1. Cut the meat into smaller, similarly sized pieces, or ask your butcher to do it.
2. Pat the meat thoroughly dry.
3. Put all the meat into one large mixing bowl.

<Callout title="Preparation clarification">

The original pork notes included a cold-water rinse with a little salt. That rinse is omitted here: washing raw meat can spread bacteria around the kitchen. [USDA guidance on washing food](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/washing-food-does-it-promote-food).

</Callout>

## Marinating and coating

1. Add the cooking wine, black pepper, salt, and optional garlic powder to the meat.
2. Mix thoroughly and marinate **in the refrigerator for 20–30 minutes**.
3. Add the tapioca starch and egg.
4. Mix until the meat is evenly coated. Start with two eggs; add more only if the mixture is too dry.

## Frying

<Callout title="Temperature clarification">

The original notes mention both **220°F (about 104°C)** and **140–170°C (284–338°F)**. Those are different ranges: 220°F is below the stated frying range. The steps below use the **140–170°C** range from Dad’s cooking notes. Monitor the oil with a frying thermometer and adjust the heat; reused oil also needs to reach the cooking temperature.

</Callout>

1. Use a deep fryer or a large, heavy pot suitable for frying. Measure oil while it is cold and leave plenty of room for the meat and bubbling. **Do not automatically pour in the whole 3 L jug**; follow your equipment’s fill limit. Keep the equipment dry and never leave hot oil unattended.
2. Heat the vegetable oil into the **140–170°C** range, checking with a frying thermometer.
3. Carefully add a small batch without crowding the pot. Dad’s pot held about **14 pieces**; use fewer if your pot is smaller or the temperature drops too much.
4. After about **40 seconds**, gently separate the pieces so they don’t stick together.
5. Adjust the burner to keep the oil within the stated range. The original notes give these approximate times:
   - **Chicken wings:** about **12 minutes**.
   - **Pork ribs:** about **15 minutes**.
   - **Chicken thighs and drumsticks:** no fixed time was recorded; size matters, so check with a food thermometer.
6. Check the thickest part of each piece, away from bone: **chicken must reach 74°C / 165°F**, and **pork must reach 71°C / 160°F**. Keep cooking if needed; the timer and coating colour are not doneness tests. [Health Canada’s cooking temperatures](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/general-food-safety-tips/safe-internal-cooking-temperatures.html).
7. Lift the cooked pieces out with a suitable utensil and drain on a rack or paper towels. Allow the oil to return to the cooking range before the next batch.
8. Let the meat cool for a couple of minutes before serving.

## Family notes

- One rack of spare ribs gave Dad about **28 small pieces**, or two batches in his pot. Your cut and pot may differ.
- The quantities above are for **4 lb of meat**. Scale the seasoning and starch if you change the batch size.
- Keep the original “a little bit” approach for garlic powder — it’s optional.
